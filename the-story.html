The Story

Back to play

In 2007 I went to my first ACEIA teaching conference, in Sevilla. In the opening plenary Tessa Woodward talked about the stages of a teaching life, and she called the first three years Survival and Discovery.

At the end of those three years of Survival and Discovery I started a blog called DUST. I had Discovered and Uncovered, Survived and been Tested. For years it has been where I have kept ideas and activities that I like. Some have come and gone, but others remain.

By the third stage, years 7 to 18, and after stabilisation, the dust I had created slowly turned to stars. That didn't come without the odd passing black hole, of course. Now here I am, in year 20. Put enough stars together and you will find light, and that's where creatively I now sit: on the coast of light.

COAST of LIGHT play is many of these classroom ideas, rebuilt as tools (with the help of Claude) to use projected in class. The focus is often on random words, which I've long been a fan of using to create sentences. My students have helped me user test it. Maybe you can too.

  1. Years 1 to 3 Survival and discovery A painful or easy start, and the shock of the real classroom.
  2. Years 4 to 6 Stabilisation Growing confidence, a style of your own, and the choice to stay.
  3. Years 7 to 18 Experimentation Trying new materials, groupings and ideas. For some, doubt and thoughts of leaving.
  4. Years 19 to 30 Serenity More relaxed and self-accepting. For some, holding on to the past.

The first four stages of Michael Huberman's teacher career cycle (1989), as summarised by Tessa Woodward.

What's here?

Tools for playing with words and ideas, and for starting conversations: random pickers, question makers, wordlists, sentence builders and games for a whole class.

I make them for my own English classes. They go up on the board and the class thinks, talks or writes. Many began as DUST posts or as activities on paper, and have been rebuilt here so they can be played again and again.

How is it made?

One page at a time. I bring an activity and what I know about how it works in a real classroom, build the page in conversation with Claude, an AI assistant, and keep changing it until it plays right. Then it goes into class, and usually gets changed again.

Whose is it?

Everything here is my own creation unless a page says otherwise. Some games are inspired by ones I have met elsewhere over the years, and I wouldn't know who to credit. If an idea came from another site, the page says so. If it doesn't and you think it should, let me know.

The Coast!

COAST of LIGHT takes its name from the Costa de la Luz, the stretch of the Cádiz coast in Spain where I live and teach. It is a creative project of colour and craft that is building slowly, and you can find out more about it on the blog.
